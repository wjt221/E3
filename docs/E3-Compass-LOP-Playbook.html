LOP Operating Guide

How to use Compass to keep a Member focused on the few priorities that will create the most enterprise value.

This is not a software manual. It is the working rhythm for an E3 Lead Operating Partner. Compass helps you remember the business, challenge the current story, find the relevant E3 thinking, and walk into each conversation knowing what matters.

01 · Purpose

What Compass is for

Compass exists to answer one question: What should I be focused on right now to help this business materially increase enterprise value?

It does that by keeping four things connected over the life of an engagement: what E3 knows, what we know about the Member, what has actually happened in the business, and what the LOP is seeing that has not yet made its way into a formal plan.

E3 knowledge

Our operating point of view, Plays, tools, questions, patterns and lessons from other engagements.

Member context

The economics, people, goals, plans, board materials, financials, constraints and history of this specific business.

Meeting evidence

Granola notes, commitments, decisions, shifts in the story, and what keeps coming back unresolved.

LOP judgment

Your instincts, questions, concerns and hypotheses. Compass records them. It does not turn them into facts.

Compass should make a strong LOP sharper. It should never make the LOP a passenger.

Claude is the reasoning layer. It is not the source of truth. The source of truth is the Member evidence and the approved E3 knowledge behind it.

02 · The role

The LOP's job

The LOP is the quarterback of the value creation engagement, not another member of the management team.

Your job is to help the Principal and leadership team identify the few things that matter most, make the hard tradeoffs, and keep moving those priorities forward. Sometimes that means challenging. Sometimes diagnosing. Sometimes bringing in expertise. Sometimes staying out of the way.

ModeWhat it looks like
ChallengeTest an assumption management has accepted too quickly.
DiagnoseSeparate the symptom from the actual constraint before recommending a fix.
FacilitateGet a decision made when the team has been circling it.
Hold accountableClose the loop on an owner, date and outcome that was already agreed.
GovernMake sure the right decisions sit with management, the board or the Principal.
Bring expertisePull in the right E3 or outside resource when the issue needs deeper capability.
MonitorKeep an issue visible without turning it into a priority too early.
Stay outLet management own an operating decision that does not need the LOP.

The test is simple: the company should become more capable because of the engagement, not more dependent on the LOP.

03 · The center of Compass

The Value Creation Thesis

Every Member should have a current, concise point of view on how value will be created and what is standing in the way.

The thesis is not a project list. It should tell you where the economic opportunity is, what capabilities or decisions are constraining it, and the three to five priorities that deserve scarce management attention.

Too tactical

Implement CRM. Hire a VP Sales. Create a board. Improve reporting. Review compensation.

A thesis

The business has strong demand but is not converting growth into proportional EBITDA. Pricing discipline, management capacity and commercial execution have not scaled with revenue.

What that thesis might drive

1
Improve incremental margins.
Understand where growth is leaking between gross profit and EBITDA, then fix the largest drivers.
2
Reduce CEO dependency.
Move recurring decisions into the leadership team and close the most important capability gaps.
3
Build a repeatable commercial operating system.
Get clear on pricing, pipeline quality, conversion and ownership before simply adding sales capacity.

Compass should challenge the thesis as new evidence arrives. It should not rewrite it after every meeting. A new topic is not automatically a new priority.

04 · Working rhythm

How to use Compass in the engagement

The best use of Compass is not constant use. It is disciplined use at the moments where memory, pattern recognition and preparation matter most.

Before a meeting
Review the current thesis, open decisions, commitments, your observations and the latest relevant evidence. Decide what you need from the meeting before you walk in.
In the meeting
Be in the room. Listen for new evidence, contradictions, avoided decisions, leadership behavior and commitments. Do not turn the conversation into a live Claude session.
After a meeting
Drop in the Granola notes. Ask Compass what actually changed. Capture decisions and commitments. Update the thesis only when the evidence warrants it.
Quarterly
Step back from the meeting cadence. Ask whether the priorities are still the right ones, what is creating value, what is stalled, and what should come off the list.

The 10-minute pre-meeting routine

  1. Ask Compass for the current thesis and top three priorities.
  2. Review commitments that are late, deferred or repeatedly discussed.
  3. Ask what evidence does not fit the current story.
  4. Pick the three to five questions worth asking.
  5. Decide what should be different by the end of the meeting.

After the meeting

Do not ask for a summary. Ask what changed. A good post-meeting analysis separates facts, management claims, observations, hypotheses, decisions and commitments. It should also tell you when nothing material changed.

05 · E3 knowledge

Use the business problem to find the Play

LOPs should not browse Compass like a handbook. Start with the problem in front of the company. Compass should bring the relevant E3 knowledge to you.

What you are seeingCompass may draw from
Founder is still the decision bottleneckPeople, Governance, Management Systems, Compensation
Revenue is growing but EBITDA is flatStrategic Finance, Pricing, Operations, GTM, Procurement
Management wants to accelerate M&ACapital Allocation, M&A, People, Integration, Management Systems
The company has too many prioritiesStrategy, Management Systems, Governance, People

An E3 Play is a repeatable intervention. It might be a Founder Decision Rights Reset, Leadership Capability Assessment, KPI Architecture, Board Creation, Pricing Diagnostic, Capital Allocation Framework or Acquisition Thesis.

Do not reach for a Play because the label sounds familiar. First establish the problem, the evidence behind it, the management owner, and what success would look like. Diagnose before prescribing.

06 · Outputs

What gets shared

Compass produces two kinds of work: candid internal thinking for the LOP and clean operating material that can be shared with the Member.

OutputUse
LOP Pre-Meeting BriefInternal. Thesis, priorities, evidence, questions, commitments at risk and what to push.
Post-Meeting AnalysisInternal. What changed, what did not, decisions, commitments, contradictions and candidate updates to the thesis.
Member Value Creation UpdateShareable. Priorities, why they matter, owners, decisions, milestones and next 30 days.
Value Creation ScorecardShareable when useful. One-page view of priority, owner, status and next proof point.
Quarterly Value Creation ReviewShareable. A strategic reset on whether the thesis and priorities still hold.
Internal LOP view

Founder dependency remains a material constraint. Pricing exceptions and senior hiring decisions still come back to the CEO.

Member-facing

Increase leadership leverage. Move recurring pricing and hiring decisions to the appropriate executives so the CEO can stay on the highest-value priorities.

Member-facing material should be candid, constructive and useful. Raw hypotheses stay internal until the LOP has validated them and decided how to frame them.

07 · Working prompts

Prompts worth using

The best Compass requests sound like a good Operating Partner thinking out loud. Ask it to compare, challenge, remember and connect. Do not ask it to decorate or summarize.

Prepare me for tomorrow's Member meeting. What are the three things I should care most about, what changed, what is at risk, and what should I push on?
What has materially changed in our understanding of this business in the last 90 days?
What evidence does not fit our current Value Creation Thesis?
What has management discussed repeatedly without actually resolving?
Which of our current priorities has the weakest evidence behind it?
Where are we treating a symptom as the root cause?
If we could only make meaningful progress on three things this year, which would create the most enterprise value?
Growth is strong but EBITDA is not following. Show me where the operating leverage appears to be leaking and what evidence supports each hypothesis.
Where is the founder still creating unnecessary dependency? Which decisions should no longer reach them?
Which parts of the three-year plan require capabilities the company does not have today?
Does the company have the management capacity and integration capability to make acquisitions accretive right now?
What are we spending management attention on that should probably be deferred or stopped?

08 · Guardrails

How Compass goes wrong

Most failure modes are not technical. They happen when the LOP gives up judgment or lets the system create more process than value.

Do not confuse a summary with analysis. Ask what changed and why it matters.
Do not let the latest meeting reset the priorities. Recency is not importance.
Do not promote every issue. Protect the top three to five priorities.
Do not accept a Claude hypothesis as fact. Compass proposes. The LOP verifies.
Do not become management. Strengthen the team rather than taking over its work.
Do not add process for process's sake. Keep only the cadence and tools that improve decisions or execution.
Compass remembers, connects and challenges. The LOP reads the room, builds trust, makes the tradeoffs and decides when to push.

Start here

  1. Review the Company Context and current Value Creation Thesis.
  2. Load the latest meeting evidence and any important financial or strategic materials.
  3. Ask Compass what the three highest-value priorities appear to be and what evidence supports them.
  4. Identify what is still assumption rather than fact.
  5. Prepare the questions that will most improve your understanding of the business.
