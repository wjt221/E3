LOP Operating Guide

How E3 Lead Operating Partners use Compass to focus management on the priorities that create enterprise value.

Compass keeps the Member's operating history, current priorities, meeting evidence and E3 knowledge in one working context. The LOP uses that context to prepare better questions, test the current thesis, track commitments and decide where to spend time.

How Compass is used

Compass helps the LOP maintain a current view of the business across meetings, financial results, plans, people decisions and prior commitments.

The system pulls from four sources. E3 knowledge provides the operating point of view and relevant Plays. Member context holds the facts and history of the business. Meeting evidence captures what management is saying and doing over time. LOP observations preserve judgment that still needs to be tested.

Claude connects those sources and helps the LOP prepare. Source documents, verified company data and approved E3 material remain authoritative.

The LOP's role

The LOP helps the Principal and leadership team identify the few priorities with the greatest effect on enterprise value and keeps those priorities moving.

That work changes by situation. The LOP may challenge an assumption, diagnose an underlying constraint, facilitate a decision, hold an owner accountable, strengthen governance, bring in expertise, monitor an issue or leave an operating decision with management.

LOP modeTypical use
ChallengeManagement has accepted an assumption without enough evidence.
DiagnoseThe team is reacting to a symptom and the underlying cause is still unclear.
FacilitateA material decision has stalled or ownership is unclear.
Hold accountableAn agreed commitment needs an owner, date and clear completion standard.
GovernA decision belongs at a different level, or management and board responsibilities are blurred.
Bring expertiseThe issue requires deeper functional or specialist support.
MonitorThe issue deserves attention but has not earned priority status.
Stay outManagement can handle the issue without LOP involvement.

Management retains responsibility for execution, operating decisions and people management. The LOP should leave the company with stronger management capability and clearer decision making.

Value Creation Thesis

Each Member should have a short, current view of where value can be created, what is limiting that value creation and which priorities deserve management attention.

The thesis should be economic and causal. A list of projects is usually too shallow to guide the engagement.

Project list

Implement CRM. Hire VP Sales. Create a board. Improve reporting. Review compensation.

Value Creation Thesis

The company has strong demand but is not converting growth into proportional EBITDA. Pricing discipline, management capacity and commercial execution have not scaled with revenue.

Priority 1: Improve incremental margins. Determine where profit is leaking as revenue grows and address the largest drivers.

Priority 2: Reduce CEO dependency. Move recurring decisions into the leadership team and close the capability gaps that keep pulling work back to the CEO.

Priority 3: Build a repeatable commercial operating system. Clarify pricing, pipeline quality, conversion and ownership before adding more sales capacity.

New information should change the thesis only when it materially changes the evidence. Frequent discussion alone is not a reason to promote an issue into the top priorities.

Working rhythm

Compass is most useful around the points where the LOP needs to prepare, remember or reassess.

Before a meeting
Review the current thesis, open decisions, commitments, LOP observations and the latest relevant evidence. Decide what you need from the meeting.
During the meeting
Stay in the conversation. Listen for changes in facts, contradictions, avoided decisions, leadership behavior and new commitments.
After the meeting
Add the Granola notes, analyze what changed, update decisions and commitments, and revise the thesis only when the evidence supports a change.
Quarterly
Review the thesis from a distance. Test whether the priorities still deserve management attention and whether any work should be stopped, deferred or elevated.

Before the meeting

  1. Review the current Value Creation Thesis and the top three priorities.
  2. Check late or repeatedly deferred commitments.
  3. Look for evidence that conflicts with the current thesis.
  4. Select the questions that will improve the LOP's understanding or force an important decision.
  5. Define the one or two outcomes the meeting should produce.

After the meeting

Meeting analysis should separate facts, management claims, observations, hypotheses, decisions and commitments. If the meeting did not materially change the view of the business, record that and move on.

E3 knowledge and Plays

The business problem determines which E3 knowledge is relevant. A single issue often crosses several functional areas.

SituationRelevant E3 areas
Founder remains the decision bottleneckPeople, Governance, Management Systems, Compensation
Revenue is growing while EBITDA is flatStrategic Finance, Pricing, Operations, GTM, Procurement
Management wants to accelerate M&ACapital Allocation, M&A, People, Integration, Management Systems
The company has too many active prioritiesStrategy, Management Systems, Governance, People

E3 Plays provide a repeatable intervention when the diagnosis is sufficiently clear. Examples include a Founder Decision Rights Reset, Leadership Capability Assessment, KPI Architecture, Board Creation, Pricing Diagnostic, Capital Allocation Framework and Acquisition Thesis.

Before recommending a Play, confirm the problem, the evidence behind the diagnosis, the management owner and the measure of success. Some situations need more diagnosis before the team starts work.

LOP and Member outputs

Internal analysis can be candid and provisional. Member-facing material should reflect validated priorities, decisions and commitments in language the LOP is comfortable owning directly.

OutputPurpose
LOP Pre-Meeting BriefCurrent thesis, priorities, evidence, questions, decisions and commitments that need attention.
Post-Meeting AnalysisChanges in the evidence, decisions made, commitments created and possible changes to the thesis.
Member Value Creation UpdatePriorities, owners, decisions, milestones and the work expected before the next review.
Value Creation ScorecardOne-page view of priority, owner, status and next milestone when the engagement benefits from a scorecard.
Quarterly Value Creation ReviewPeriodic review of the thesis, progress and changes in priority.

Internal LOP analysis

Founder dependency remains a material constraint. Pricing exceptions and senior hiring decisions still return to the CEO.

Member-facing language

Move recurring pricing and hiring decisions to the appropriate executives so the CEO can spend more time on the company's highest-value priorities.

Useful prompts

The most useful requests ask Compass to compare evidence, identify patterns, test assumptions and prepare questions.

Prepare me for tomorrow's Member meeting. What are the three issues I should care most about, what changed, what is at risk, and what should I push on?
What has materially changed in our understanding of this business in the last 90 days?
What evidence conflicts with our current Value Creation Thesis?
What has management discussed repeatedly without resolving?
Which current priority has the weakest evidence behind it?
Where might we be treating a symptom as the root cause?
If we could make meaningful progress on only three things this year, which would create the most enterprise value?
Revenue is growing but EBITDA is not. Where does the operating leverage appear to be leaking, and what evidence supports each explanation?
Which decisions still reach the founder that should sit elsewhere in the organization?
Which parts of the three-year plan depend on capabilities the company does not have today?
Does the company have the management capacity and integration capability to make acquisitions accretive right now?
What is consuming management attention that should be deferred or stopped?

What Claude reads

Compass works from a defined set of files. The answer changes depending on the question, but Claude should always start with the Member's current context before reaching into broader E3 knowledge.

  1. Verified Member evidence
    Financials, plans, board materials, source documents and meeting records.
  2. Current Member view
    Company Context, Value Creation Thesis, open decisions, commitments and LOP observations.
  3. Approved E3 knowledge
    Capability files, approved Plays, tools and templates that fit the issue being analyzed.
  4. Cross-Member learning
    Patterns and case learning that have been approved for reuse.
  5. Claude reasoning
    Used to connect the evidence and form hypotheses. Claude should identify when an idea comes from reasoning rather than established E3 IP.

Example: how Compass would use ARI context

ARI illustrates how company evidence and E3 knowledge work together. The example below uses ARI context already available to E3. It has not been validated against a Dropbox source in this session.

Member context Claude may know

Long-term ambition: become the most recognized name in technically complex drilling and blasting in the U.S., with a prior goal of $250 million of revenue and $1 billion of enterprise value.

Economic guardrail: avoid risk projects and maintain at least a 20% gross margin.

Operating constraint: operator utilization and productive hours are a major focus, with a 40% to 50% productive-hour goal.

Organization: leadership structure, project management capacity and compliance ownership have been active issues.

Files Compass would reference

01_Company_Context.md for ARI's business model, goals, leadership structure and economic guardrails.

02_Current_Value_Creation_Thesis.md for the current three to five priorities.

Meeting_Intelligence/ for recent Granola discussions about operators, EPC customers, risk projects and org design.

Reference_Materials/ for the current plan, financials, org chart and any operational data loaded for ARI.

A question from the LOP

“Where should I push Katie and the team if we want to scale ARI without sacrificing margin or taking on the wrong work?”

Additional E3 knowledge Claude may pull

OPS_Operations.md for utilization, capacity and operating leverage.

PPL_People_Organizational_Capability.md for leadership capacity and role design.

MGT_Management_Systems.md for priority ownership, KPIs and accountability.

FIN_Strategic_Finance.md for margin discipline and economics by project/customer type.

Likely Compass focus: determine whether the constraint is demand, operator capacity, project selection, project management leverage or organizational design. The answer should use current ARI evidence to distinguish among those explanations before recommending a Play.

Operating discipline

Compass is useful when the LOP treats it as a working record and a source of challenge. It becomes less useful when every new issue becomes a priority or every generated hypothesis is accepted without verification.

  • Ask what changed, not for another meeting summary.
  • Keep the top priorities limited enough that management can act on them.
  • Verify Claude's hypotheses against the company evidence.
  • Keep execution ownership with management.
  • Use E3 Plays after the diagnosis is clear enough to justify them.
  • Retire processes and outputs that do not improve decisions or execution.

Starting a new Member engagement

  1. Review the Company Context and available financial, strategic and organizational materials.
  2. Load the latest meeting evidence.
  3. Draft an initial Value Creation Thesis and identify which parts remain assumptions.
  4. Prepare the questions that will most improve the LOP's understanding of the business.
  5. Update the thesis as evidence develops during the first 100 days.
